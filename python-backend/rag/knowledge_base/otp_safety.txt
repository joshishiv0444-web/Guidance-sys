== OTP Safety Guide ==

What is an OTP?
OTP stands for One-Time Password. It is a temporary 4 to 6 digit code sent to your registered mobile number or email to verify your identity during a transaction or login.

When is an OTP sent?
- When you log into a banking app or website.
- When you make an online payment.
- When you change your password or PIN.
- When you add a new beneficiary for fund transfers.

How to use an OTP safely?
1. Never share your OTP with anyone — not even bank employees, family, or friends.
2. Enter the OTP only on the official app or website that requested it.
3. An OTP expires quickly (usually within 2-5 minutes). If it expires, request a new one.
4. If you receive an OTP without requesting any transaction — alert your bank immediately.

Common OTP Scams to Watch Out For:
- Scammer calls pretending to be a bank employee and asks for your OTP.
- Scammer says they need your OTP to "verify" or "update" your account.
- Fake SMS links that take you to a fake banking page asking for your OTP.
RULE: NO legitimate bank or company will EVER ask for your OTP.

What to do if you accidentally share an OTP?
1. Call your bank's customer care number immediately.
2. Block your card or account from the banking app if possible.
3. Change your UPI PIN and banking passwords right away.
